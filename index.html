Current thesis

The next frontier in government is not AI for its own sake. It is applied intelligence embedded into public systems: procurement, permitting, urban management, service delivery, land systems, census operations, health and disaster response, and the digital institutions that make states more capable.

What this site is for

A home for essays, books, and practical thinking on how cities and governments can become smarter, faster, and more equitable, without losing sight of political economy, implementation realities, or institutional design.

Current Agenda

Three arguments shaping the work right now

This site is organized around a practical intellectual agenda: what governments need to rethink if they want AI to improve public outcomes rather than simply add new technology to old systems.

Argument 1

AI only matters when it changes how the state works

The real prize is not a pilot or a chatbot. It is better execution inside procurement, permitting, urban management, service delivery, and the internal operating systems of government.

Argument 2

Cities are where abstract technology becomes real governance

Cities sit at the junction of land, infrastructure, politics, delivery, and citizen experience. That makes them the most consequential proving ground for public-sector AI.

Argument 3

State capacity is the missing variable in most AI debates

Data, institutions, incentives, financing, and administrative capability determine whether digital tools strengthen the state or simply expose its weaknesses more quickly.

About

Public policy, technology, housing, and urban infrastructure

Abhas Jha is an Indian public policy expert and author working at the intersection of digital technology, urban infrastructure, affordable housing, and public services. He is the World Bank's manager for urban development and disaster risk management in Eastern and Southern Africa.

Before joining the World Bank in 2001, he served for twelve years in the Indian Administrative Service. His work has since spanned more than 40 countries across Asia, Africa, Latin America, and the Caribbean, combining policy reform, development finance, institutional design, and implementation.

His urban work includes affordable-housing and urban-poverty programmes in Mexico; infrastructure, land, and resilience programmes across several regions; and research on integrated urban flood-risk management. He is a lead author of Cities and Flooding and Safer Homes, Stronger Communities, and a co-author of Building Urban Resilience.

His current writing and public work examine how artificial intelligence, satellite imagery, open mapping, digital twins, and digital public infrastructure can improve government decisions and deliver better services, especially for low-income and climate-vulnerable communities.
